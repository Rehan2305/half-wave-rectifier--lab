Aim

To study the operation and characteristics of a half-wave rectifier, observe its input and output waveforms, and calculate its key performance parameters.

01 / CONCEPT

Theory

01

A half-wave rectifier is a diode circuit that passes one half of an AC waveform and blocks the other. The resulting output is unidirectional but pulsating. A resistive load converts the output current into a corresponding voltage.

Positive half-cycle

The source makes the diode anode positive relative to its cathode. The diode is forward biased and conducts once the input exceeds its forward drop. Current flows through the load.

Negative half-cycle

The source polarity reverses. The diode is reverse biased and blocks current, so the ideal load voltage is zero. The diode must withstand the peak input voltage.

Key relationships ideal diode, sinusoidal input

Peak outputVm(out) ≈ Vm − VD
Average (DC)VDC = Vm(out) / π
RMS outputVRMS = Vm(out) / 2
Ripple factorr = √[(VRMS/VDC)² − 1] ≈ 1.21
Rectification efficiencyη = PDC/PAC = 4/π² ≈ 40.6%
Peak inverse voltagePIV = Vm

For a practical silicon diode, the simulation subtracts an approximate 0.7 V forward drop. The ripple factor and efficiency shown are calculated from the simulated waveform.

Single-diode circuit

Conventional current flows during the positive half-cycle.

03 / LAB METHOD

Procedure

03
  1. Study the circuit.Identify the AC source, series diode, and resistive load in the circuit diagram.
  2. Set the controls.In Simulation, choose an input peak voltage, frequency, and load resistance.
  3. Observe conduction.Compare the input and output waveforms. Note when the diode conducts and when it blocks.
  4. Record readings.Read peak, average, RMS, ripple factor, efficiency, and PIV. Add a row to the observation table.
  5. Verify the trend.Change one control at a time and observe how the waveforms and measurements respond.
  6. Complete the posttest.Use your observations to answer the questions and review explanations.
